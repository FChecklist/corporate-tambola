# Requirements register: privacy, gaming, marketing-claim and related law

Research document, not legal advice. Prepared 2026-10-05 for two products audited later against the same checklist:

* **T = Corporate Tambola** (organizer-run corporate tambola/housie; repo `C:\ct\tambola`).
* **D = VERIDIAN AIOS DPDP** (SaaS that helps Indian organisations run DPDP compliance; repo `C:\ct\ct-shortlink`, `dpdp-app/`).
* **both** = applies to both.

## How to read this file

* **Source codes (S01...)** point to `SOURCES.md`, which gives the URL, access date, and whether the source was a primary text, an authoritative summary, or a secondary summary. Most Indian government sites (MeitY, India Code, PRS, JSA PDF, egazette) returned HTTP 403 or refused the connection, so **the DPDP Act and Rules were NOT read from the primary text**; they were read through law-firm, consultancy and encyclopaedia summaries. **Update 2026-10-05 (audit, Step 0):** the Act sections 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 16 and the Rules 1, 3, 6, 7, 8, 10, 14, 15 were then read verbatim from a web reproduction of the official text (dpdpa.com, source S41). That is still a secondary reproduction, not the Gazette; compare against G.S.R. 843(E) before relying on it in a legal document. The rows touched are marked 'Verified 2026-10-05'. Every DPDP entry is therefore capped at "medium" confidence unless two independent secondary sources agree. Rule numbers should be re-checked against Gazette notification G.S.R. 843(E)-846(E) before anyone relies on them.
* **Confidence**: high = primary text or several consistent authoritative sources; medium = secondary sources only, or my own knowledge consistent with them; low = inference, single weak source, or my own recollection not verified in this session.
* **Applies to**: ORG = the organizer / the customer organisation (usually the data fiduciary / controller / "business"); PLAT = the platform operator (IT Services by Rajat Agarwal per `legal.html`) acting as processor / service provider, and as fiduciary / controller for its own account, partner and billing data.
* **Column "T / D"**: which product the row matters for.
* "Open question" cells are things a lawyer must decide; they are not findings against the products.
* IDs are stable. Never renumber; add new rows at the end of a group.

## 10. Open questions needing a lawyer (roll-up)

1. Is a voluntary corporate game inside DPDP s7(i) "employment purposes", s7(a) "voluntarily provided", or does it need consent (IN-06)?
2. Does the Rule 6 / Rule 8(3) one-year log retention conflict with a 19-hour participant-data deletion (IN-08, IN-10)?
3. Is a free, pure-chance tambola with organizer-funded prizes outside the "online money game" definition, and is an application for a determination under the PROG Rules 2026 worth making (GAME-IN-01 to 03)?

## 11. Ten highest-risk areas for a product like this (my judgement, not a finding about the code)

1. **Participant is charged anything (entry fee, ticket, donation, buy-to-play)**: turns a free game into an "online money game" (India, up to 3 years' imprisonment for offering) and a lottery in the US and UK (GAME-IN-01, US-GAM-01).
2. **Unverified privacy promises** ("no cookies", "no IP", "deleted in 24 hours", "7-day backups", "never sell"): each is an enforceable representation (MKT-04, EU-18, F01 to F03).
3. **Deletion clock vs logging duties**: 19-hour deletion vs one-year log retention in DPDP Rule 6/8(3) and 180-day CERT-In logs (IN-08, IN-10, IN-31).
4. **Minors**: "family members" and school customers; DPDP s9 and COPPA/GDPR Art 8 (IN-13, EU-05, US-02).
5. **Lawful basis in an employer setting**: consent is weak between employer and employee; s7(i) is unsettled for a voluntary game (IN-06, EU-19).
6. **Cross-border chain**: EU organizer to India platform to US vendors, India with no EU adequacy, DPF appeal pending (EU-16, EU-17).
7. **Compliance claims by a compliance vendor**: "DPDP compliant", "we sell protection", "consent manager", "attestation", "audit-ready" (MKT-02, VEN-01 to VEN-05, IN-19).
8. **AI access**: bearer-secret work links, prompt injection from participant names or inbound mail, and the "personal-data-free projection" claim (AI-02, AI-04).
9. **Winnings tax and state gambling law borne by the organizer** (TDS 30% over INR 10,000, state housie enforcement, NY/FL registration) which the platform must warn about (GAME-IN-04, GAME-IN-06, US-GAM-02).
10. **Contract gaps**: no processor DPA with CCPA terms and SCCs for organizers/customers, and no public sub-processor list (EU-10, US-CA-02, IN-21).
